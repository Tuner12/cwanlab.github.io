Lab guidelines v3.1

The full guidelines below apply to every member of the lab. Details can always be discussed with the PI based on specific situations.

Responsible research

  • The first author is fully responsible for the integrity of the project and all related aspects — e.g., making sure all data and code associated with the paper are properly maintained in the lab's shared storage (UChicago Box and GitHub) to enable reproducibility.
  • No research misconduct is permitted. Fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, inappropriate data manipulation, or any form of cheating in research practices is strictly prohibited.
  • The first author must document individual contributions; individuals who do not contribute meaningfully should not be listed as co-authors.
  • Co-first authorship is permitted only if contributions follow at least a 60%–40% distribution.
  • Collaborations and publications with others, especially institutions and individuals outside the U.S., must be approved by the PI in advance.
  • Report all conflicts of interest (COI) and conflicts of commitment (COC): finish the training and submit the COI–COC disclosure in AURA.

Failure to comply with this Responsible Research Policy may result in disciplinary actions in accordance with UChicago policies, including, but not limited to, removal from authorship, reporting to institutional oversight bodies, and, in severe cases, termination from the lab.

Expectations for postdoctoral researchers

The primary responsibility of postdoctoral researchers is research: publishing papers (~70%) and writing proposals (~30%) together. The following are general minimum expectations.

  • Submit at least two first-author major papers annually (see Appendix), with at least one accepted first-author major paper per year.
  • Fully participate in proposal submissions — prepare at least one major proposal (Direct ≥ $0.5M; mainly NIH R01) together each year.
  • Apply for postdoctoral fellowships (university, foundation, federal), such as ACS, AACR, and NIH K99/R00.
  • Mentor undergraduate and graduate students in research.

Generally, a grant application needs one publication plus preliminary results.

Bonus. Acceptance of papers in journals with impact factor ≥ 20 is eligible for a bonus (e.g., vacation flexibility, equipment purchase).
Bonus. Successful grants totaling ≥ $0.5M (Direct) are eligible for a bonus (e.g., salary increase).

Requirements for Ph.D. graduation

  • Fulfill all program requirements (e.g., qualification exam).
  • Graduate within 4.5 to 5.5 years.
  • Minimum publications are listed in Table 1 — generally at least three major papers.
  • Papers cannot be written into the dissertation as main components if the PI is not the senior/co-senior author.
  1. Year 1If there are no paper submissions within the first year, we revisit the research plan together.
  2. Year 2By the end of the second year, submit at least one first-author major paper.
  3. Year 3By the end of the third year, have at least one first-author major paper accepted and at least one additional first-author major paper submitted.
  4. RAFor students supported as RAs, continued RA funding depends on research progress — typically at least two first-author major paper submissions annually after the second year.

Vacation

  • Vacations are encouraged after major paper/proposal submissions, and discouraged in the run-up to important deadlines (conference deadlines, NIH Standard Due Dates).
  • For those supported by research funding (e.g., RA), up to one week of vacation per quarter is typically reasonable. Postdoctoral researchers follow the UChicago Postdoctoral Researcher Policy Manual.
  • Publishing CCF-A papers or journal papers with IF ≥ 15, or securing grants totaling ≥ $0.5M, may offer additional flexibility.
  • Ph.D. students are encouraged to focus on research early. Industry internships are generally recommended only after publishing at least two major papers.

Appendix · What counts as a major paper

Paper quality is evaluated using the China Computer Federation (CCF) A/B/C classification (ML / CV / NLP / AI, HCI, Database / DM / IR, and others). Conferences included in CSRankings by default are considered CCF-A equivalent (e.g., RECOMB, ISMB). Journals are evaluated by impact factor; we aim for journals with IF ≥ 5 or IEEE/ACM Transactions (IF does not matter).

A major paper is generally (1) a peer-reviewed CCF-B main-conference full paper, or (2) a peer-reviewed CCF-B+ / IF-5+ or IEEE/ACM Transactions journal paper.
